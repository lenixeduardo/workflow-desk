Make vs Zapier vs n8n: Choosing an Automation Tool for a Small Business

Compare three automation approaches by ease of setup, hosting responsibility, maintenance and practical fit.

The short answer

Choose based on the workflow you need to operate, not a headline about the number of integrations. Make offers a visual scenario builder, Zapier emphasizes app-based workflow setup, and n8n offers a workflow platform with cloud and self-hosting options. Product capabilities and pricing change, so confirm current terms before purchasing.

Make: visual control

Make can be a good fit when you want to inspect how data moves through a multi-step process. Its visual editor can make branching logic and field mapping easier to discuss with teammates. Check usage-based costs, polling intervals and connector permissions before committing.

Zapier: straightforward app workflows

Zapier can suit teams that want to connect common business apps with a relatively direct setup process. Evaluate whether the specific triggers, actions and advanced logic you need are supported on your intended plan.

n8n: more operational control

n8n may appeal to technically capable teams that want more flexibility and the option to self-host. Self-hosting adds responsibility for uptime, security, backups, updates and credentials. Do not assume it is cost-free once hosting and maintenance are included.

A fair evaluation checklist

Build the same small test in each shortlisted tool. Record setup time, available connectors, error handling, monthly usage estimate, data handling, maintenance burden and how easy it is for another team member to understand the workflow.

Our practical recommendation

If your team is new to automation, start with one internal notification workflow using the tool you can maintain. Make is worth testing for its visual editor, but a different tool may be better for your technical requirements or budget.

What to do next

Create a sample scenario using fictional data, confirm it handles duplicates and failures, then compare the actual effort. Avoid paying for an annual plan before verifying the complete workflow.
