Make vs Zapier vs n8n: Choosing an Automation Tool for a Small Business

Compare three automation approaches by ease of setup, hosting responsibility, maintenance and practical fit.

The short answer

Choose based on the workflow you need to operate, not a headline about the number of integrations. Make offers a visual scenario builder, Zapier emphasizes app-based workflow setup, and n8n offers a workflow platform with cloud and self-hosting options. Product capabilities and pricing change, so confirm current terms before purchasing.

Make: visual control

Make can be a good fit when you want to inspect how data moves through a multi-step process. Its visual editor can make branching logic and field mapping easier to discuss with teammates. Check usage-based costs, polling intervals and connector permissions before committing.

Zapier: straightforward app workflows

Zapier can suit teams that want to connect common business apps with a relatively direct setup process. Evaluate whether the specific triggers, actions and advanced logic you need are supported on your intended plan.

n8n: more operational control

n8n may appeal to technically capable teams that want more flexibility and the option to self-host. Self-hosting adds responsibility for uptime, security, backups, updates and credentials. Do not assume it is cost-free once hosting and maintenance are included.

A fair evaluation checklist

Build the same small test in each shortlisted tool. Record setup time, available connectors, error handling, monthly usage estimate, data handling, maintenance burden and how easy it is for another team member to understand the workflow.

Our practical recommendation

If your team is new to automation, start with one internal notification workflow using the tool you can maintain. Make is worth testing for its visual editor, but a different tool may be better for your technical requirements or budget.

What to do next

Create a sample scenario using fictional data, confirm it handles duplicates and failures, then compare the actual effort. Avoid paying for an annual plan before verifying the complete workflow.

Compare using the same practical test

Use one controlled scenario for all three platforms: a new fictional enquiry in Google Sheets generates a notification to an internal email inbox. Do not compare one tool's basic workflow against another's advanced implementation.

  1. Write down the trigger, required fields, filter, notification and failure behavior.
  2. Record how many setup steps are needed to authorize accounts and map fields.
  3. Test a valid row, an incomplete row and a duplicate entry.
  4. Inspect execution history and error messages for failed runs.
  5. Estimate recurring costs based on actual executions, checking each provider's current billing definitions.
  6. Ask a second team member to explain the workflow from its editor and documentation.

Decision framework

Choose Make to evaluate when...

You want a visual representation of multi-step routing and are comfortable monitoring usage and scenario runs. Start with a simple prototype before assuming that it will be cheaper than alternatives.

Choose Zapier to evaluate when...

Your priority is connecting familiar business apps with a straightforward setup. Confirm that your required triggers, filters, paths and error-handling features are included in the plan you would actually use.

Choose n8n to evaluate when...

Your team has the technical capacity to customize workflows and, if self-hosting, manage updates, backups, credentials, uptime and security. Self-hosting shifts operational responsibility to your team.

Cost comparison without misleading numbers

Do not compare a headline monthly price without checking how each platform counts tasks, operations or executions. For your specific workflow, document trigger frequency, steps per execution, retries, data retention, hosting and staff maintenance. Compare total expected cost for the same usage pattern.

Frequently asked questions

Which platform is best for beginners?

It depends on the apps and logic involved. Try the same small workflow in the available trial or free options and compare your own setup experience.

Is self-hosting automatically free?

No. Infrastructure, monitoring, backups and maintenance have costs even when the software itself can be run without a subscription.

Should I migrate an existing workflow immediately?

Not without documenting dependencies and testing error cases. A migration can interrupt business processes even if the new editor appears simpler.
