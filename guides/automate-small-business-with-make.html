How to automate your small business with Make

Start with one repetitive task, not a complicated system. This walkthrough shows how to use a spreadsheet to trigger a simple follow-up workflow, while keeping a person responsible for customer communication.

What should you automate first?

Choose a task that happens repeatedly, follows clear rules and has an easy way to verify the result. For example, when a team adds a new enquiry to a Google Sheet, Make can notify the person responsible for following up. That removes the need to check the sheet manually throughout the day.

For your first project, use a notification to your own inbox rather than sending automatic messages to customers. This avoids accidental duplicate messages and gives you a safe place to test.

What you need

  • A Google account with access to Google Sheets.
  • A Make account and permission to connect your Google account.
  • A test spreadsheet with columns Created at, Customer, Request, and Status.
  • An email inbox you control for receiving test notifications.

Step-by-step: spreadsheet to email notification

  1. Prepare the spreadsheet. Create a Google Sheet called Enquiry Tracker with the four headers above. Add one fictional test entry; avoid real customer data while experimenting.
  2. Create a scenario in Make. Open Make, select Create a new scenario and add a Google Sheets module that watches for new rows. Connect your Google account and select the spreadsheet and sheet.
  3. Choose a starting point. Configure where the watch module should begin reading. For a clean test, start with new rows added after setup, if that option is available in your configuration.
  4. Add an email step. Add a supported email module, such as Gmail, to send a message to your own address. Set a descriptive subject like New enquiry requires review and map the customer and request fields into the email body.
  5. Run a controlled test. Use Make's test/run option, then add a new fictional row to the sheet. Check whether the scenario detected the row and whether the notification arrived. Depending on your trigger and schedule settings, detection may not be immediate.
  6. Review before enabling. Confirm that a single row results in the intended notification, that blank or malformed rows are handled, and that no customer-facing messages are being sent.
  7. Enable and monitor. Turn on scheduling only after the test succeeds. Revisit the scenario history periodically for errors and usage.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Automating a broken process: clarify who owns each enquiry before automating handoffs.
  • Sending duplicate emails: test retries and watch-module starting points.
  • Ignoring privacy: connect only the accounts and data you are authorized to process; avoid placing sensitive information in notifications.
  • Skipping monitoring: a scenario can stop working when an account connection expires or a spreadsheet structure changes.
  • Assuming instant delivery: scheduled polling and provider limits may delay notifications.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Make provides a visual scenario builder. Understanding data fields, conditions and error handling still helps.

Can I automate customer replies?

Potentially, but start with internal notifications. Before sending customer messages, check consent, applicable marketing rules, duplicate prevention and the content of each reply.

Will this make my business run automatically?

No. It automates a defined handoff. People still need to review enquiries, handle exceptions and maintain the workflow.

Build and verify the scenario

  1. Set the Google Sheets watch module to the correct spreadsheet and worksheet. Choose the starting point deliberately to avoid replaying older records.
  2. Add a filter requiring non-empty Customer and Request fields.
  3. Connect a supported email module to your own test inbox. Map the row's Customer and Request fields into a clear internal notification.
  4. Run the scenario using fictional rows and inspect the execution history. Confirm that one valid row produces the expected notification.
  5. Test an incomplete row, a duplicate entry and an expired connection. Document the observed results before turning on scheduling.

Cost and troubleshooting checklist

Estimate how often the trigger checks for new rows, how many modules run per record and how retries affect usage. Compare your expected activity with the current Make pricing. If nothing arrives, inspect the selected sheet, trigger starting point, filter, connected accounts and email spam folder. If duplicates arrive, check retries and repeated source records.
