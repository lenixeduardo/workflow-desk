Build a Simple Lead Tracker with Google Sheets and Make

A practical guide to collecting, reviewing and routing leads without losing track of follow-ups.

Start with a clear workflow

For a small service business, a spreadsheet can be enough to organize new enquiries. The goal is not to automate sales conversations: it is to make sure a responsible person sees each enquiry and can follow up.

Prepare your spreadsheet

Create a sheet with these headers: Created at, Lead ID, Name, Contact, Request, Owner, Status, Last reviewed. Use fictional data for testing. Assign a unique Lead ID to help prevent duplicates. Avoid storing sensitive information you do not need.

Connect Make

In Make, create a scenario with a Google Sheets module that watches for new rows. Select the appropriate spreadsheet and worksheet, and choose a starting point that avoids accidentally processing older rows. Add a filter to continue only when required fields are present.

Notify the right person

Add a supported email or team-messaging module. Map the Lead ID and Request into an internal notification. Initially, send notifications only to your own test inbox. Do not automatically contact the lead until you have confirmed consent, appropriate wording and relevant rules.

Test failure cases

Add a test row, check the scenario run history and verify that exactly one notification arrives. Then try an incomplete row and a duplicate Lead ID. Make sure these do not trigger an inappropriate follow-up. Review the impact of retries and connection failures.

Measure usefulness

Track how long it takes someone to review a lead, the number of missed enquiries and how often a notification fails. If the process is reliable, document the owner, troubleshooting steps and expected monthly usage.

Before you scale

Review access permissions, privacy requirements, and the current limits of your Make plan. A spreadsheet is not a replacement for a CRM when your team needs advanced permissions, audit history or complex sales reporting.
